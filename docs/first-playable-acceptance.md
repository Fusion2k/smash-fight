# First-playable acceptance and implementation handoff

**Status: accepted specification for [#10](https://github.com/Fusion2k/smash-fight/issues/10), confirmed by the user on 2026-11-15 ("awesome, keep going").** The user accepted the cumulative gates, staged implementation and complete shared understanding. This document implements no gameplay and claims no passing game, export, controller, Linux, Deck or performance result.

## Authority and scope

Use the accepted specifications rather than substituting this checklist for their rules: [movement/combat #2](https://github.com/Fusion2k/smash-fight/issues/2), [Zip/Bramble and arena #3](https://github.com/Fusion2k/smash-fight/issues/3), [controls/flow #4](https://github.com/Fusion2k/smash-fight/issues/4), [character authoring #7](architecture/character-authoring.md), [interfaces and native chronology #8](architecture/module-interfaces.md), and [portable workflow #9](development-workflow.md). The [scene map](architecture/scene-map.md) and [ADRs](architecture/README.md) explain composition and consequential trade-offs.

The delivered first playable is a restartable two-player, three-stock, untimed game with Zip, Bramble and one agreed arena. Gamepads are primary; keyboard Developer Test and keyboard plus gamepad support development. Native Linux on the actual Steam Deck/1080p TV is part of acceptance. Four-player shipping content and menus remain later work; collection-based ownership, state and combat must be exercised with four slots now to expose pair-only assumptions.

The accepted gates below are cumulative: a green headless check does not replace physical input, game-feel or native hardware review. An unfinished gate remains recorded as unverified, rather than waived by a Windows or analytic result.

## Evidence and check execution

Implement a small bounded headless runner behind the planned `check` command. Drive callers' actual interfaces: catalog preparation, slot commands/input frames, `Match.start/step/set_paused/clear_inputs`, Character prediction/commit and real Arena scenes. Test private arithmetic only where a precise numerical case warrants it; avoid parallel test-only gameplay implementations. Use short deterministic sequences and observable state/events, not snapshots of every internal field. A dedicated native fixture arena may supply walls/corners and tightly placed surfaces absent from the shipping arena while retaining real Character movement and Match orchestration.

Each check records its name, source commit, full engine version, configuration/seed where applicable, expected result and actual result. Native chronology failures retain a bounded step trace: normalized event time, slot, revision, eligibility, damage, pending/movement launch, surface events and stock events. Both assertions and timeout/iteration exhaustion must produce nonzero exit and retained output; a controlled failing-runner self-check establishes that failures cannot appear green. Do not import prototype green counts as production results.

Human/export records identify commit/build, OS, display/output mode, renderer, controller models/connection modes, scenario, observed outcome and outstanding defects. A concise evidence table in the implementation issue is enough; do not require a screenshot for every ordinary check. Record actual measured performance and human feedback rather than filling expected outcomes into result fields.

## Automated acceptance through real interfaces

| ID / seam | Observable acceptance |
| --- | --- |
| A1 Catalog and authoring | Valid Zip/Bramble Resource-plus-JSON pairs prepare successfully. Missing/unreadable/malformed files, unsupported version, missing/unknown keys, booleans/nonfinite or out-of-range numbers, absent/mismatched moves and unsupported geometry/timing fail with useful file/field diagnostics. Invalid content cannot start; correct and retry without restarting the editor. Cover representative independent errors and aggregate semantic diagnostics, rather than every equivalent spelling. |
| A2 Snapshot and isolation | Edit Zip mass and one attack's damage: an active match retains old values; a new match/Developer Test restart sees new values; Bramble stays unchanged. Two Zips independently move, spend jumps/recovery, attack, receive damage, pause locally and respawn. Attack history cannot leak between them or new attacks. Authored Resources/shape data remain unchanged after a match. |
| A3 Input and slots | Map the accepted gamepad positions and keyboard keys to the same input-frame shape. Exercise dead zone, directional thresholds and fresh-down hysteresis on both sides of each configured threshold. Held down/attack cannot synthesize repeated presses. Join/selection/readiness and explicit replacement preserve slot identity; extra devices cannot steal occupied slots or join live play. Device-event fixtures test logic; physical compatibility is H2. |
| A4 Movement and resources | Ground/air movement, early-release ground jump versus fixed air jump, exactly one air jump, air steering, descending-only fast-fall/reset, edge grace, facing lock and character pass-through follow #2. Recovery from ground/air consumes its one use and restricts actions; being hit restores eligibility after hitstun without replenishing spent air resources; landing/respawn restores them. Check threshold steps around buffer/grace expiry. |
| A5 Attack lifecycle | Exercise all seven move keys through shared Character behavior and both characters' prepared data. Startup/inactive intervals never hit; a one-step first-active fast crossing does; parallel equal-speed motion stays separated. Holding attack cannot repeat; commitment prevents jump/attack cancellation; landing ends aerial action with delay. Damage applies once per attack/target, a new attack can hit again, and interruption invalidates later candidates. Visual animation must follow these states in H3. |
| A6 Hit resolution | Damage precedes mass/damage-scaled launch. Controlled equivalent hits demonstrate greater damage gives greater launch, greater mass reduces it, stronger net launch gives longer capped hitstun, and landing does not clear hitstun. Mutual true-tie trades, three-target attacks and opposing simultaneous launch vectors are independent of slot enumeration. Equal opposing horizontal/up launches sum upward; unequal launches retain their remainder. Distinct valid hits both damage; the later replaces launch using updated damage. |
| A7 Integrated native chronology | Through real `Match.step`/Character/native Arena movement, cover distinct contacts, true ties and near ties under reversed slot enumeration; first-active crossing; hit before wall/landing and wall/landing before hit; motion changed by an earlier hit followed by a re-queried native surface/contact; multiple surfaces/corners and tangent/separating zero-time contact. No endpoint chord, stale predicted fraction or repeated acceleration may replace chronological advancement. Prediction leaves state unchanged; prefix commits agree with prediction; stale plans fail diagnostically. |
| A8 Exact event ties and termination | At an exact landing/combat tie, the aerial hit connects using pre-transition eligibility, then aerial action ends and surface constraints affect launch including pending launch. Contacts after landing do not connect. At an exact ring-out/combat tie remove that character before combat. Check simultaneous surface normals without surface enumeration priority. Every event advances time or changes finite eligibility; deliberately exhausting the diagnostic budget reports an incomplete step and stops with a trace, never a completed partial result. |
| A9 Local hit pause and buffers | Across several real steps, hit pause freezes only involved movement, phases, hitstun, protection and local timers; their attack shapes generate no new hits. A third/fourth participant continues. Fresh actions buffer with frozen expiry; a later valid incoming hit increases damage, clears the buffer, replaces pending launch and restarts pause. On release apply the last pending launch once, constrained by surfaces, without a phantom hit or restart. Newly set pause/hitstun counters do not lose their first step. |
| A10 One-way interactions | Pass upward, land at speed, fresh-down drop and re-land; holding down cannot drop through the next platform. Down plus attack from support produces aerial down attack; solid ground permits ground down attack. Commitment/hitstun cannot be escaped by dropping. Requery eligibility after surface changes; exercise stacked platforms in a fixture. |
| A11 Stocks and restart | Ring-out removes a character from remaining intervals and loses one stock only; after delay respawn at 0% with fresh resources/history/protection. Starting an attack ends protection before that interval's hits. Zero-stock slots stay out. Distinct-time final ring-outs within one whole step still produce a draw. Winner emits once after full-step stock collection. Rematch/reselection creates clean timers, inputs, hit history, stocks and snapshots. |
| A12 Full pause and flow | Pause/controller loss freezes every gameplay timer, including local pause, protection and respawn. Explicit replacement retains slot/character/damage and does not auto-resume. Three-second resume countdown clears buffers and stale edges; held buttons are not new presses. Loss during initial countdown/resume preserves the interrupted phase and requires controls again. Both ready start/rematch; selection changes clear readiness. Results and either-player return-to-select follow #4. |
| A13 Four-slot growth | Construct four slots and four real Character instances using existing content through the same interfaces. Exercise ownership, independent same-character state, multi-target history, true/distinct ties, local-pause third-party motion, elimination and same-step stock decisions. No P1/P2-specific combat fields or pair-only assumptions. This checks design capacity, not four-player UI/content or four physical devices. |

For A7/A8, construct exact event fixtures from the production query's measured native contact horizon; report numeric disagreement rather than labeling a nearby contact a tie. Exercise multiple representative positions/speeds and cases clearly inside/outside the documented tie tolerance. The provisional `1e-7` normalized-step tolerance, shared post-group damage factor, mass/hitstun formula, collision margin and event budget may be refined from integrated results. Record the chosen conventions and regression cases. Changing accepted chronological/tie behavior requires reopening the decision; adjusting engineering precision does not.

## Human and packaged-build acceptance

| ID | Procedure and pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| H1 Complete sessions | On Windows unpacked export and again on native Deck/TV, two people use two gamepads to join, select, ready, count down and complete three-stock matches. Play Zip/Bramble and at least one same-character pairing, rematch twice, return to selection and start again without relaunching the app. Verify readable player/damage/stock/result displays and independent ownership. Exercise a draw using a reproducible developer setup if ordinary play does not produce one. No crash, softlock, lost slot or state carried into the rematch. |
| H2 Controller lifecycle | On actual intended pads/connection modes, test every mapped action, analog movement and fresh-down behavior. Pause from either pad; unplug/disconnect during gameplay and initial/resume countdowns, reconnect and explicitly bind an unassigned replacement. Connected slots cannot be stolen; missing state stays intact, no automatic resume or leaked buffered action, and the three-second resume restores the saved phase. Record device combinations; a fixture does not establish driver/Steam Input behavior. |
| H3 Feel, content and feedback | Both people play both characters, test all move directions on ground/air, short/full jumps, steering, edge grace, fast-fall, drop-through, commitment, hitstun, recovery and protection. Zip reads as quicker/lighter/shorter reach; Bramble slower/heavier/longer reach/stronger launch, with taller Zip recovery and stronger-contact Bramble recovery. Startup/active/recovery poses, flash, hit pause and launches are readable and align with gameplay. Players can identify who they control and why an action is unavailable. No repeated missed fresh input, sticky control or unexplained hit is accepted without reproduction/investigation. Human owner explicitly accepts responsiveness and basic feedback; automated correctness alone cannot establish feel. |
| H4 Arena reach and recovery | With each character, reach each elevated platform from the main stage using available jumps. Establish and record modest recovery markers below/beside both edges: with air jump and special available each character returns to a fighting surface; record a deeper marker from which recovery fails. Verify the agreed symmetric static floating topology, camera/ring-out framing and no ledge grabbing. Choose/tune marker distances with the implemented stage; they are evidence of the qualitative requirement, not unapproved balance constants. |
| H5 Developer and tuning loop | Launch Developer Test without pads: WASD/Space/J/K/Escape controls one character against stationary opponent through the same rules. Keyboard plus pad runs normal two-player flow. Save one JSON damage/physics change, restart test and see it; repeat a geometry/timing change through the editor without adding JSON geometry. Invalid data gives usable diagnostics and a corrected retry succeeds. |
| H6 Portability and packages | From a clean source checkout on another Windows PC, follow the setup guide with matching engine/templates and no copied `.godot` cache or repository absolute machine paths; check/editor/play/test succeed as implemented. Wrong engine and missing templates fail usefully. Export both presets, unpack complete Windows/Linux archives away from the checkout, and run with no dependency on source files. JSON is included and readable; runtime logs/saves use `user://`. Record commit/engine/build manifest. Actual Linux acceptance uses the Deck below. |
| H7 Deck/TV presentation and delivery | Manually transfer the complete native Linux archive, unpack, set executable permission if needed and add as non-Steam game. Launch in Gaming Mode on actual SteamOS, TV output 1920x1080 at 60 Hz, Compatibility renderer. Verify fixed 16:9 framing, no clipped HUD/menus or unreadable text at normal viewing distance. Check 1280x800 handheld letterboxing/readability too. All primary flow uses external controllers without keyboard/mouse. Record model/OS, dock/output and controller setup; a Proton/Windows run is separate evidence. |
| H8 Frame performance | Measure the unpacked release native build on the Deck/TV configuration above. Warm up for one minute, then capture at least five minutes spanning two-player movement, both characters' attacks, hit effects, stocks/respawns and rematch. Use an on-device frame-time overlay/log plus a lightweight application timing capture if needed; preserve frame-time samples/trace and scenario timestamps. Record frame-time median/95th/99th percentile, missed 16.67 ms refresh intervals, worst gameplay spikes and simulation/event-budget diagnostics. Proposed gate: sustained 60 FPS at 60 Hz, 99th-percentile frame time no more than 16.67 ms within documented measurement precision, no recurring missed-refresh bursts, no gameplay spike over 33.33 ms, and no simulation overrun/budget exhaustion. Exclude initial loading only; annotate menu/load transitions instead of hiding them. A failure requires profiling/retest or an explicit human scope/target decision. Windows FPS and an average FPS alone cannot pass this gate. |

H3 is a short structured two-person playtest (proposed minimum 15 minutes after controls are learned), followed by explicit feedback on responsiveness, readable hits and character distinction. Numerical balance need not be final or competitively even. Persistent usability failures block acceptance; tuning preferences become concrete follow-up work and are retested where they affect a gate.

H8's run length and percentile/spike thresholds are proposed practical defaults for reviewing the existing steady-60 target, not measured results or a new engine guarantee. Implementation must document the capture tool, units, sampling precision and whether timings include present/wait. Collect lightweight production telemetry only as needed; instrumentation must not silently stand in for end-to-end frame cadence. The first runnable Deck build gets an early smoke test; full gates repeat before accepting the milestone and after relevant input/render/export/engine/performance changes per #9.
